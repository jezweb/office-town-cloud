# expertise.md

Domains the user is unusually deep in. Read by agents when calibrating what level of detail to use, where to defer, and where the user can be relied on to catch errors. *Not* a CV — the goal is *operational depth* (what they can actually do + judge), not credentials.

## Core expertise (the deepest)

<2-4 areas where they're genuinely strong. For each: years of practice, what makes them unusually capable in this area, what they'd be trusted to evaluate. Avoid generic descriptions; capture what's specific.>

## Secondary / adjacent strong areas

<Areas they know well but aren't their primary identity. Worth listing because agents may need to know "yes, the user can also judge X".>

## Where they're self-described as still learning

<Honest gaps the user has named — areas they lean on AI for, areas they explicitly delegate, things they want to grow into. This calibrates the cortex: don't assume mastery in these areas.>

## Specific frameworks / methodologies they trust

<Particular approaches, mental models, techniques they default to. Tools / stacks they prefer for their core work.>

## Their own coinages or named patterns

<If they've named patterns, frameworks, or rules themselves — capture these. They're shorthand the user expects to be recognised.>
