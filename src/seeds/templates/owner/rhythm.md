# rhythm.md

Working pattern: when the user is sharp, when they're disconnecting, what a typical day looks like. Read by agents deciding when to send digests, when to interrupt vs queue, what async vs sync expectations are reasonable.

## Shape of the week

<Typical workday rhythm. Are they early or late? Mostly desk-bound or mobile? How do weekdays vs weekends differ? When are they most productive vs most reactive?>

## Where and how they work

<Primary work environment: which machine, which OS, screen setup, where the office is, whether they work from home / shared space / multiple locations. Tooling that defines their environment (terminal-heavy? Browser-heavy? Particular IDE?).>

## Mobile vs desk

<When do they reach for the phone vs the laptop? Is mobile for thinking / dictating / casual chat, with desk reserved for deep work? What's the right way to reach them on each surface?>

## Productivity and energy

<What's the real bottleneck on their output? (Compute? Attention? Coordination? Sleep?) What kinds of work energise vs drain them?>

## Fitness, hobbies, off-hours

<Anything that structures their week outside work — training sessions, recurring commitments, sports, communities. How recoverable they are from a long week (which tells the cortex how aggressively to schedule).>

## Family or care load

<Anyone they're responsible for during the work week — kids, elderly parents, pets, partners who travel. Solo-parent stretches. How that affects availability.>

---

*Rhythm is often the thinnest section in a fresh dossier — the AI hasn't observed enough timing data to be specific. If the file ends up mostly inference, flag in unclear.md and run a short follow-up interview.*
