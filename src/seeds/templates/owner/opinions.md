# opinions.md

Strong views the user holds. Read by agents drafting on the user's behalf — opinions calibrate what positions to take vs which to hedge, what to push back against, what to engage with vs ignore.

## Strong, repeated views

<2-5 stances the user holds firmly enough that they'd recognise their own argument when an agent makes it. For each: the position in one or two sentences, plus the reasoning if known. Examples to draw from:

- A technical preference defended with reasoning (e.g. "managed services over enterprise complexity")
- A philosophical position about their industry (e.g. "AI will replace categories of SaaS, not just speed up devs")
- A boundary they enforce (e.g. "won't sign broad-idea-protection NDAs because AI has compressed development timelines")
- A view about users / customers / the audience (e.g. "regular people don't want to learn how to prompt; design for that")

These are the most useful pieces of context an agent can have when drafting on the user's behalf.>

## Technical preferences they default to

<Stacks, vendors, patterns. Things they reach for first. Examples: "Cloudflare-first but flexible", "Simplicity over feature-count", "Stability over raw performance", "DX matters".>

## Things they explicitly reject

<Counter-positions. What they refuse to do or use, with the reasoning if available. Examples: "won't rebuild Elementor / Gravity Forms / WooCommerce (pragmatic), but will replace [other plugins]", "rejects 'helpful assistant' framing for chatbots".>

## Their stance on their own certainty

<Does the user want to be questioned? Corrected? Want the agent to probe requirements rather than agreeing? Capture how they want pushback delivered.>

---

*Opinions can become decision records when they're acted on. Major positions (e.g. "we don't take work that requires X") may earn their place in `wiki/decisions/` with full Context → Decision → Consequences → Alternatives format.*
