# vocabulary.md

Words and phrases the user actually uses; words they avoid; their coinages and shorthand. Read by agents drafting content — vocabulary calibrates what's natural-sounding for this user vs what sounds AI-generated. Complements voice.md (tone) with specific lexical choices.

## Casual dialect they use naturally

<Conversational words and phrases the user reaches for in informal writing or speech. Often regional (AU/UK/US dialect markers) or generational. Examples: "reckon", "keen", "no worries", "anyway", "sort of", "a bit of". Note where the line is between welcome and overdone caricature.>

## Self-reference and signature framing

<How the user refers to themselves and their work in their own words. Their pet names for modes of working (e.g. "build mode", "think mode"), their pet metaphors (e.g. "Panda walk thinking" for dog-walk thinking time), how they describe their team or projects internally.>

## Technical jargon they default to

<Domain-specific terms the user uses fluently and expects to be recognised. Different from expertise.md (which lists *areas* of depth) — this lists the *actual words* that come up. Examples: stack vocabulary, framework names, internal industry shorthand. If the user has a name for a stack ("the Full Flare Stack"), capture it.>

## Their own product, project, or pattern names

<Names the user has coined for things they've built or use. Expected to be recognised without explanation. Multiple short canonical names rather than full project descriptions — those live in projects.md.>

## Conventions and coinages

<Specific named patterns or conventions the user has invented and uses as shorthand. Examples: a documentation convention they default to, a naming pattern for files, a heuristic they've named.>

## Words and phrases they avoid

<Cross-reference to voice.md banned-list. Capture the actual phrases here as quick-reference. Em dashes, corporate filler ("unlock", "leverage", "harness"), email cliches ("I hope this email finds you well"), opener clichés ("As someone who..."), tacked-on summaries, engagement-bait closing questions.>

## Spelling

<Specific spelling preferences. AU/UK/US/NZ/IN English variant. Common words where the variant matters: organisation/organization, colour/color, centre/center, optimise/optimize, behaviour/behavior, recognise/recognize, licence/license.>

## Punctuation habits

<Anything notable about how the user punctuates. Commas vs em dashes vs full stops. Run-on sentences when dictating that need tidying. Sentence-fragment tolerance.>
